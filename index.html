The Second Weekly Budget has failed to receive approval from the All-Union Congress of Soviets. Effective immediately, the first official government shutdown is declared. During the shutdown, sectors will not receive budget allocations, and employees will not be paid for work completed until the budget dispute is resolved. No back pay will be issued for work performed during the shutdown period. Sector leaders are instructed to inform their workers of the shutdown and to cooperate with Congress and the Finance Commissariat in negotiating a final budget settlement.

Quick Reference

The National Money Cycle

Rubles spent on State fees and approved store items return to the treasury. Weekly national budgets then fund sectors according to activity, productivity, approved contracts, and decisions of the Congress. Sector spending and attachment maintenance return money to the treasury again.

Current Political Register

Political Situation

This register identifies the currently published political parties, their leadership, ideological positions, platforms, and recorded membership within the Union.

MSPSU · Conservatives

MorSoc Party of the Soviet Union

Party General Secretary: Gen. Sec. Kewl Menzinsky
Party Ideology Paternalistic Conservatism
Party Platforms Social Conservatism, Mixed Economy, Isolationism

The Conservative Faction is one of the main political movements within the Moralistic Socialist Party of the Soviet Union (MSPSU), dedicated to the preservation, protection, and continuation of the Party's original ideological stance, principles, and mission. Its followers say that the strength of the server lies not in constant reinvention/changes, but in adherence to the values upon which the Moralistic Socialist Revolution was founded. Conservatives believe that every institution of the USSR, from the @Central Committee of the MSPSU to the smallest role, exists as the product of years of sacrifice, discipline, and struggle from our leaders and "forefathers". They view fast political experimentation, changes, sweeping structural reforms, and ideological 'deviation' as the greatest threats to stability, arguing that servers are rarely destroyed by external enemies, but by abandoning the things that made them strong in the first place. The faction prefers measured, disciplined, and carefully supervised changes, ensuring that any necessary reforms strengthen rather than diminish the foundations of Moralistic Socialism. They uphold the authority of the Party and it's leadership, organizational discipline, historical continuity, and ideological orthodoxy. It draws its support primarily from Party veterans, founding era members, experienced admins, senior military and security officials, and long serving sectoral leadership.

MSPSU · Reformists

MorSoc Party of the Soviet Union

Party General Secretary: Gen. Sec. Kewl Menzinsky
Party Ideology Pragmatic Modernization
Party Platforms Social Conservatism, Mixed Economy, Isolationism

The Reformist Faction is one of the main political movements within the Moralistic Socialist Party of the Soviet Union (MSPSU), dedicated to the belief that the strength and "longevity" of the U.S.S.R depends upon its ability to adapt intelligently and rapidly to changing historical, economic, technological, and realities. Reformists say that while the principles of Moralistic Socialism are eternal and are supreme, the methods through which those principles are realized must "evolve" or "change" alongside the server they serve. They argue that institutions like the @Central Committee of the MSPSU and servers which refuse to 'modernize' eventually lose the capacity to defend the party, and that genuine loyalty to the Party requires the courage to confront head on, the inefficiency, stagnation, corruption, and outdated practices wherever they arise. This faction primarily promotes pragmatic modernization. Reformists seek more "responsive" governance, greater administrative efficiency, technological advancement, stronger engagement with the concerns of the Soviet citizens, and the refinement of Party institutions to meet the demands of each new 'generation'. Rather than viewing reform as a departure from the Party's ideals, Reformists say that the adaptation is highest expression of those ideals, asserting that the Revolution was never intended to stand still, but to endure. The faction draws its support primarily from younger Party members serving in sectors most affected by stagnation or institutional inefficiency.
